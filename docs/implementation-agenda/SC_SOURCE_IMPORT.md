# Security Council voting source: inspected first real import

The official UN Dag Hammarskjold Library Security Council Voting Data, Version 7 (October 2026), was downloaded on 23 October 2026 from the [UN Digital Library catalog](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4055387). The [CSV](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4055387/files/2026_10_16_sc_votes.csv) is 15,561,944 bytes, SHA-256 `c73e8d7b61ee1323e8a48af2fcbb5b8d14ab05619030862793a79ecfb0cbb5c2`. Its [metadata](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4055387/files/2026_10_16_sc_voting_md.md) is 3,805 bytes, SHA-256 `99a1c3a952ccf1cfb33c876fc0c24e1dd9a55797aebb12ad6d1d5e9a7a2eb534`. Raw files, normalization output and reconciliation receipts remain in private staging outside the public repositories.

The metadata covers adopted resolutions through resolution 2829, dated 29 September 2026. It excludes paragraph votes and drafts that failed adoption. The downloaded CSV contains 41,559 country-resolution rows; its earliest recorded row date is 25 January 1946. The title dates resolution 1 to 24 January 1946, so this adapter does not silently rewrite the observed earliest row. In the frozen window 24 October 2016–23 October 2026 there are 7,740 rows across 516 resolution groups. This is adopted-resolution voting coverage, not comprehensive UN voting, national issue positions or statement coverage.

The first bounded import selects the newest 33 complete resolution groups: 495 rows, each group reconciled against the publisher's country count and Y/N/A/X totals. Normalized categories are 457 yes, 37 abstentions and one unknown. The next group cursor is 33. The batch is explicitly incomplete. The cursor must be bound to the exact source hash and date window; it is not a universal ingestion watermark.

After that first successful batch, normalization continued through 16 bounded batches and all 516 resolution groups. All 7,740 rows reconcile with published totals: 7,381 yes, 281 abstentions, 77 unknown/non-voting and one no. All recorded country codes in this window join the repository's exact ISO3 registry; the canonical name, recorded historical name, code and registry hash remain separate. Normalized private batches and the complete reconciliation ledger were handed to the sole database writer. Full normalization is verified; database persistence has its own ingestion receipt. None of these votes has been assigned an issue stance.

`research/issue_history/ingest/un_sc_csv.py` validates the exact Version 7 headers, official record links, adopted resolution symbols, historical recorded names/codes, dates and categories. Four actual source resolution symbols contain a space before the year. Normalization removes that separator space while retaining the original symbol separately. Duplicate countries, conflicting metadata and incomplete group totals stop the batch. Each batch is capped at 500 rows and never splits a resolution group.

The dictionary defines X as non-voting, which does not establish absence or non-eligibility. It becomes unknown with eligibility unset. More significantly, the dictionary says resolutions adopted without a vote or by acclamation may carry Y. Such rows must not become individual affirmative ballots. The adapter retains the raw code and modality but emits unknown for every `Without Vote` row. A normal roll-call Y/N/A establishes a recorded yes/no/abstention and eligibility. Country names remain as recorded, with the three-letter country code retained; historical naming cannot be repaired by capitalization alone.

All normalized votes remain policy-unbound. A full-resolution vote cannot establish agreement on an isolated humanitarian clause or an explicit national statement. Resolution-topic bindings require separately inspected primary text, source links and an honest review state; assistant review is never owner confirmation. The source terms specify non-commercial use with attribution and applicable UN conditions. This implementation records provenance and makes no new public raw-data release.

The complete English primary texts of resolutions [2417 (2018)](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1627380/files/S_RES_2417_%282018%29-EN.pdf?version=1) and [2730 (2024)](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4049572/files/S_RES_2730_%282024%29-EN.pdf) were separately inspected for provisional topic relevance. Resolution 2417 addresses humanitarian access in operative paragraphs 4 and 6, and civilian protection in paragraphs 1, 5 and 7. Resolution 2730 addresses access in paragraphs 8–9 and protected personnel/civilian objects in paragraphs 3 and 6. Four assistant-provisional bindings, primary PDF hashes and paragraph/page anchors remain in private staging. Their context is thematic armed conflict, not a particular geographic situation.

The exact corresponding CSV records contain 15 affirmative votes for resolution 2417 on 24 May 2018, and 14 affirmative votes plus one abstention for resolution 2730 on 24 May 2024. The U.S. has a recorded affirmative vote in both. This supports whole-resolution voting comparisons only. It does not establish national positions on every provision, contemporary policy continuity or exact agreement on humanitarian access or civilian protection in a different situation. The PDF hashes are `6f03ce306aff2ddedebd149a561f9d8ae78562aee6e2f7e5827c67eb02e086bb` (2417) and `48a8c79b45322a58bd1c9beb1580f7802f5d6349ca1d4051f5f17379b884571e` (2730).

Successful execution on this real bounded source verifies this adapter path, not scientific model validity or complete ten-year issue coverage.
