# Expanded policy coverage · 1.21.0

The automatic country-first report now checks 30 policy questions, up from seven. Policy and country dropdowns remain unnecessary. All questions found in explicit evidence appear in the country summaries, trajectories, daily evidence tables, JSON, CSV and script-free HTML exports.

The 23 additions cover the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty; IAEA Additional Protocol; Biological Weapons Convention; Chemical Weapons Convention; Arms Trade Treaty; UN Programme of Action on Small Arms; nuclear disarmament; Treaty of Pelindaba; fissile material cut-off treaty negotiations; Paris Agreement; climate finance; Loss and Damage Fund; 2030 Agenda and SDGs; a treaty protecting persons in disasters; International Criminal Court; death-penalty abolition; a global death-penalty moratorium; Security Council reform; Security Council expansion; recognition of the State of Palestine; UNRWA; Korean Peninsula denuclearization; and removal of Cuba from the U.S. terrorism-sponsor list.

Named international policy questions use the stable `global` context identifier. That identifier includes named institutions and explicitly international objectives, not only treaties. The original regional questions still require a matching context, and Cuba measures require Cuba-specific evidence. Removal from the U.S. terrorism list additionally requires an explicit United States/U.S./US reference in the same sentence. The small-arms Programme of Action requires small-arms/light-weapons wording; unrelated UN programmes cannot satisfy it. Support for an NPT Additional Protocol or NPT Review Conference cannot by itself establish support for the NPT.

All labels describe expressed direction on the bounded question. Support for a treaty does not establish implementation, ratification or compliance; support for an agency or fund does not establish a financial pledge. Agreement on the broad direction does not mean agreement on every provision, amount or implementation detail. Reported positions, merely descriptive text, ambiguous attribution, changes to a different object and unresolved wording remain withheld or qualified. The rules do not claim general stance accuracy or exhaustive coverage.

Only matched policy/context/calendar dates contribute to U.S. comparisons. Repeated statements add no weight, missing evidence does not count as disagreement, and prior statements are not carried forward. Distinct questions may be related; comparison counts are not independent statistical observations.

Automatic context discovery first identifies contexts with admissible evidence, then builds the corresponding comparisons. It no longer scans every possible context for every policy in the report builder. Source provenance and the established comparison rules are preserved.

Validation includes per-policy synthetic positive, negative, conditional, reported-speech, wrong-object and automatic-discovery tests; NPT/Additional Protocol and unrelated Programme of Action boundaries; an explicit Cuba/list jurisdiction check; group-attribution abstention; different-policy baseline gaps; duplicate-invariant weighting; and browser save/reopen of new-policy evidence, CSV and standalone HTML. The retained October 14–16 collection was replayed privately, preserving original records, hashes, notes, exclusions, filters and meeting groups. All reserved October 5–6 transcripts remain unopened. No external AI calls.
