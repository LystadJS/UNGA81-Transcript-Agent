# Browser release 1.17.1

Reviews and refines the provisional humanitarian discourse labels. Handles continuing calls and explicit urgent-need / essential-action phrasing; distinguishes observed access barriers from prescriptive references; excludes bare commitments and broad aid discussion; labels reported concrete activities separately from requests. Quoted or reported appeals, detention-monitor access, institution-name matches and unclear transcript wording remain visible as review flags rather than implied direct demands.

Collective delivery requires recorded group metadata or a first-person delivery cue. Mere alignment, thanks to another speaker, and national-capacity remarks do not establish group delivery. Conflicting national and group cues produce a qualified mixed/ambiguous attribution. No verified governmental position is inferred.

The new method is humanitarian-discourse-v2. Frozen v1 rules authenticate old saved label snapshots against their retained records before the current rules recompute labels. Saving exports v2 snapshots; the original-file download retains the imported bytes. Tampered snapshots still fail closed. Original transcripts, grouping metadata, filters and numerical denominators are unchanged.

Validation: expanded synthetic classification, quotation, negation, concrete/future action, institutional/detention scope, group-conflict, v1 migration and tampering checks; fresh-browser old-to-new saved-label and filter recovery; 13 collection recovery and 13 latent comparison browser checks; public build. A private assistant-provisional review inspected matching language and context in 106 development passages; it is not owner-confirmed gold, a held-out evaluation or a general accuracy claim. No private source text or annotations are published.
